FREEDIE

A game for Android about knocking down buildings with a hammer, on a summer street of a seaside town, and then out on the winter ocean.

0.58 · early days · no release yet

The street, drawn live

drag to look around

This is not a video. Your browser draws the street here with the game's own shaders, the same ones the phone runs: first the sun's depth map, then the sky and some six thousand boxes into a frame 274 pixels across, then that frame onto the screen with every pixel a hard square.

The boxes are one frame the game wrote out on the desktop: houses with shutters, balconies and awnings, palms of blocks whose fronds let the light through, the promenade and the sea, parked cars, the plinth. Drag across, and the eye turns as it does in the game, and swings back when you let go. The red button on the lamp post glows; in the game, three presses on it open the dispatcher's bridge.

The frame is an older one than the game is now.

What the page cannot do: the cars stand still here, and nothing breaks. That needs the game itself.

How it plays

Every game opens with Freedie on the street, a giant of brick, stone and planks in a maid's dress; the eye rises to her, the name comes up over her in gold, and the eye goes into hers: from then on you see the street as she does.

A building on the plinth in the middle of the road, and the hammer in your hand: drag to aim, hold still to wind up, let go to throw. Five stars, one a throw; three quarters of the building off the plinth before they run out. A building felled at one throw brings a few throws of the heavy hammer; lose the same one five times and the golden hammer is yours, one throw that scatters it whatever it hits.

Bring a building down and the dispatcher's room closes in over the street: the street stays on the tube of an old set, the real clock and date above it, the level below. Your hammer knocks the keys on the console, the hammer given if there is one, and last the one that goes on; the set flicks over, the room draws back, and the next building is already coming down. The evening goes on as the levels do, into the night; after the 101st a ring of cloud opens over the plinth, and a hammer thrown through it takes you out onto the winter ocean.

Three presses on the red button and you sit down at the set: its channels are the stretches of the game you have reached, every level in them open to play again.

How it is made

  • Java 8 and the platform alone, no build system, no libraries.
  • The game lives in plain Java: rules, physics, what stands where. Each frame it fills a draw list, the eye, the sun, the sky and every box in the scene, and a renderer draws it.
  • On the phone that renderer is OpenGL ES 3; on the desktop stand and on this page it is the same shaders in a browser.
  • Everything you see is boxes; everything you hear is made by a synthesizer in the game.
