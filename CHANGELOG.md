# Changelog

## 0.33
- A film between the first episode and the second: out of the light, a tipper truck backs
  up the road to the plinth, beeping, its beacon turning, and stops with a hiss of air. Its
  body rises on the ram and the load slides out row by row under the swinging tailgate:
  steel drums, old tyres, road cones, the things the second episode builds with. They fall
  for real, tumbling and knocking on the plinth and the road. The body comes down and the
  truck pulls away in a puff of black smoke. The first time it plays whole; after that a
  touch cuts it short.
- New sounds: a diesel, a reversing beeper, air brakes let off.
- The films between episodes share one frame now, so the next one is a matter of its own scene.

## 0.32.1
- Gold as fire: the golden cube on a building, the cube the crane brings and the golden
  hammer's head burn from inside now, white-hot in the middle of a face, deep orange at the
  rims, with slow tongues of brightness rising over them. The cube on a building breathes a
  warm haze round itself. They keep their light in the dark.
- The cubes on the console, in the bank and on the bridge, the cube button and the golden
  hammer over the buttons are orange gold too, a hot spot on the top.

## 0.32
- The golden cube for an episode only for an episode played clean: from its first level on,
  every building felled at its first throw. Lose a try, or take two throws, and the console
  knocks only the button that goes on.
- Once the golden hammer has been seen, about one building in four wears a golden cube on its
  highest free top, a glowing heart in a band of gold that glints now and then. It never
  falls to the street. Fell the building and the cube goes into the bank, once a level: on
  the console and on the bridge the bank stands at the right, its cubes behind bars. They are
  not the player's yet; the games will pay them out.

## 0.31.1
- An episode won for the first time gives a golden cube, and it is given where it is shown:
  the console's hammer knocks the cube's button and the cube goes into the rack. Until now
  the button was knocked at every episode's end and no cube came. The first one is the
  twentieth level's.

## 0.31
- A landmark that comes round again in town is never its own copy any more. Thirteen of the
  town's buildings were exact twins of earlier ones, only their colours changed: the recipes
  stop growing after the 32nd level and the seed only painted them. Now the second time a
  landmark stands it holds something else, the third time something else again, the fourth
  time it is as it was the first time, and the fifth it holds what it held the second time,
  the other way round. Balls, bottles, drums and blocks take turns; every one stands and is
  checked on the stand.

## 0.30.1
- One way out of the bridge: the key with the back arrow, the number of the level the player
  came from on it, goes back to the street as it was. HOME is gone; the red knob sits in the
  middle of the desk's front, bigger.

## 0.30
- The bridge is the console room now, the same one seen between rounds, and the player sits
  down at it. One set; the episodes are its channels, and a big red knob on the front of the
  desk clicks from one to the next, with a moment of snow. There are only as many channels as
  the game has reached, and the fight once she has come: the knob stops against the last.
- A channel not paid for comes scrambled, grey and torn into sliding bands, its slot an empty
  slit with an amber lamp blinking. A tap on the slot drops a golden cube from the tray on the
  desk into it like a token, and the picture comes clear for good.
- The green button goes into the channel: its first level, or the red street of the fight.
  The key at the left goes back one level from the one being played. HOME goes back to the
  street. The lamp sways, a fan's shadow crawls round the wall, the tube flickers.

## 0.29
- Five lost tries in a row at the same building, not ten, and the ring round the level's
  number has five segments. At the fifth the console gives the golden hammer and, in town,
  Freedie comes at once: the hammer knocks the golden hammer and then the fight. Won or lost,
  the fight ends before the same building with the golden hammer in hand, and one throw
  takes it. Out at sea she does not come yet: the golden hammer alone.

## 0.28
- No sky between rounds any more. When a building is down, the dispatcher's room closes in
  over the street from above and below, and the street goes still on the tube of an old
  set, its sides running off the frame. Over the set the clock and the date, the player's
  own, in glowing digits, and the golden cube in its slot that powers the set; under it a
  row of buttons; under those the level, big, and a key back to the level before. Above and
  below, the room goes off into the dark.
- The hammer is the hand: it comes up out of the dark, big, lazy, and knocks the buttons one
  after another, each going in with a click and lighting up: the hammer given, if one is
  (in its own colour), the cube when an episode is won, the fight when it is due, and last
  the one that goes on. A lost round goes on the same way, to the same level.
- The set flicks over with a moment of snow, the counter shows the next level, the room
  draws back off the street, and the next building is already coming down.
- About two seconds between rounds, where the sky took four or five.

## 0.27.1
- The face in the mirror comes up slowly with the first flash, pixel by pixel, as a picture
  comes up in the developer; it stays through the lightning and washes out slowly after the
  last.
- It is darker and almost grey, a little cold; the darkening leaves the brightest alone, so
  its eyes still burn. It sways with the steel, as though seen under water.
- Which face, and which way round, is chance, new at every visit; there are four of them.

## 0.27
- The wreath round the mirror is an even gradient now: black at the steel's edge, going
  over smoothly into the sky's own colour and light further out, as if the mirror drank the
  light round it. Blacker and wider when the stars ran out.
- In every flash of lightning a face shows through the mirror, pale, see-through, a little
  cold, and is gone a breath after the light: smiling for a building felled at one throw,
  calm for a few throws, stern when the stars ran out.

## 0.26.1
- The mirror is bigger, wider than the screen, cut off at its sides.
- The wreath round it no longer bristles out into the sky: the sky's blue is heaped in over
  the black in round billows of cloud, big and small, drifting slowly, their edges lit as
  the hour lights a cloud.

## 0.26
- The sky has a mirror now instead of the room with the glass floor. High over the street
  hangs a great ball of polished steel in a wreath of black storm cloud. Seen from below it
  shows the street from above, bent round as in a drop of mercury: the plinth and what is
  left on it, the palms, the houses, the sea, the horizon in a ring at its rim. It takes the
  hour and the season as the street does: a sunset in it, lit windows at night, the grey
  ice out at sea.
- In the middle of the mirror stands whoever looks up at it: a dark Freedie, a giant, her
  hammer in her hand, her eyes alight. Not plain at the first look.
- The weather judges the fall: lightning out of the wreath and thunder for a building
  felled at one throw; a far flash for a few throws; nothing for many; and when the stars
  ran out the wreath goes blacker and one bolt falls. The bolts are thick and forked, the
  frame flashes cold with them, and the near ones make the steel shiver.
- A gift comes out of the mirror itself: a ring runs out over the steel as over a liquid,
  and the hammer falls through it.
- Heavy throws add up past nine: their marks go in rows of nine under the stars, five rows
  at most.

## 0.25
- Who watches from the sky is no longer a row of old gods in the clouds. High over the
  street hangs an iron hoop, and in it a round room with a glass floor: the eye goes up and
  sees them from below, lying on the glass, heads to the middle, looking straight down. The
  stone one, the fire one, the ice one, each a Freedie of her own; a fourth place on the
  glass is left empty. Only those already met are there: the stone one from the start, the
  fire one once the golden hammer has come, the ice one after the first fight or out at sea.
- They judge the fall each in her own way, by pose and eyes: laughter, drumming on the
  glass, with lightning from the fire one's fist, for a building felled at one throw; a nod
  and a wave for a good fall; arms crossed for a poor one; and when the stars ran out, a
  fist and red eyes, a head shaken, or one who has fallen asleep.
- A gift comes from the one it belongs to: the heavy hammer from the stone one, the golden
  from the fire one, the diamond from the ice one. Her arm reaches out, the hatch in the
  middle of the floor opens, and the hammer falls through it down to the street.
- The room takes the time of day and the season: its lamps warm over the evening street,
  its glass dark under the night sky; over the winter sea the lamps burn cold and the grey
  sky lights it little.
- At night the stars keep their shape overhead, where the view now goes when it looks up.

## 0.24
- The time of day runs with the game: one long evening from the first level to the portal.
  The first episodes are as before, the sun already low and the shadows long. Then the sun
  sinks: by the fortieth a golden hour, by the sixtieth a red sunset, the light warmer, the
  colours deeper, the contrast harder. After the sixty-eighth the sun is gone, an afterglow
  over the end of the street, the lamps come on; the blue hour; and from the eighty-fifth
  on it is night, a pale moon on the right, stars, windows lit here and there along the
  houses, the shops lit below. Between one level and the next the light moves on over a
  couple of seconds. Out at sea it stays the grey winter light.

## 0.23

- Out at sea the gods give the golden hammer again where in town Freedie would come.

## 0.16
- The fight with Freedie. Once the golden hammer has been given, the next ten buildings lost
  in a row bring her instead: the gods frown and drop a diamond hammer from the thunderer's
  cloud, and the view comes down into the street seen red and black, the plinth gone, and
  Freedie far up the road, half again as tall as in the opening, burning in a golden fire.
  The fight can also be had at once from the bridge: the middle monitor of the console.
- She comes in leaps: crouches, springs, comes down so the street jolts and the dust flies,
  and between leaps tears a parked car from the curb, lifts it over her head and throws it.
  A car knocked out of the air goes up and over to the curb; one that gets through breaks
  a shield, and when the three are gone the heart is heard, and the next one ends it. Time
  runs slow while a car is about to arrive.
- The diamond hammer flies hard and flat and comes straight back. Her weak points glow: a
  knee struck goes dark and her leaps grow short; both, and she drags herself on her knees;
  her eyes go from yellow through orange to red, and the third blow to the head breaks her
  apart. A blow to her bulk knocks a brick out and stops her for a moment.
- Won: the heap trembles a while, the world goes gold and up into the sky, where all the
  gods laugh aloud and give the heavy hammer; then the next building. Lost (a car through
  with no shield left, or she reaches the player): it goes dark, and the player wakes on the
  street before the same building, the eyelids parting.
- Parked cars thrown in the fight lie where they fell as wrecks until the fight is over.

## 0.15
- The polished balls are true spheres now, worked out pixel by pixel on the graphics chip:
  each in one of six deep colours, with a paler marbled swirl and three finger holes that
  turn with the ball as it rolls, and a small hard shine of the low sun with the sky in the
  polish. Before, a ball was five round slices stacked up and read as a striped toy ball.
- Balloons are spheres too: plain rubber, no holes, a broader and softer shine.
- Balls hide and touch each other as round things do, and their shadows are round.

## 0.14
- The second episode, whole: the game goes to the twentieth building. Its things come in
  one after another, each out of boxes: steel drums with their chimes and hoops and a bung
  in the lid; car tyres, tread and sidewalls round a hole; sandbags rounded off and tied at
  one end; old suitcases with straps, brass corners, latches and a handle; spring mattresses
  between concrete slabs; heavy polished balls; balloons that drift off when freed.
- A suitcase that lands hard, or is sent flying, bursts: the lid swings up on its hinge, the
  lining shows, and shirts and postcards fly out. The shirts are as big as real ones, with
  sleeves, a white collar and a big flower print; they swing down slowly, turning over, and
  lie flat where they fall. The postcards side-slip like leaves.
- The mattresses are thick, in broad blue and cream ticking, with dark piping round the
  edges and buttons in rows; under a load the middle sinks. Struck hard, one tears: a dark
  hole with grey wadding round it, springs jumping out of it and flying off.
- Every thing has its voice: drums clang, tyres thud, sandbags thump and puff dust, leather
  slaps, a mattress whumps and creaks, latches snap, cloth tears, balloons pop, a ball
  rumbles as it rolls.
- Nobody walks on the road any more: the people who crossed the street far off are gone,
  and on the square of the opening everybody keeps short of the curbs.

## 0.13
- The parked cars take what falls on them: the hammer, blocks, boards, stones, balls. A
  light knock only clatters; a real blow crumples metal, sprays glass and sets the alarm
  off, the indicators blinking. Each blow is a step worse: crazed glass and a dented boot
  and hood; then the roof pressed lower, a side window gone, the back dented, a headlamp
  out; then the rear window gone and a door stove in. The cars stay as dented as they are.
- A parked car is a thing to aim at: the ring settles on it as on the building.
- On the right, a stretch of the curb is kept clear of parked cars: the way into a yard
  is to be there.

- On the bridge the hammer is held like a hand: a tap pokes what is under the finger, out

## 0.5

  bridge, the same room as in the game this one grows from: a ring of old sets, one for
  each episode, turned by a swipe and settling with a click; a slot under each, where a

## 0.3
- The opening: the eye flies in an arc high over the seaside town and comes down onto the
  street before Freedie, who raises her eyes; the town goes dark, light left on her alone;
  she swings her golden hammer straight at the eye, the glass cracks, and she breaks apart
  and falls, every piece of her a body of the game's own physics; on the black the name
  burns in gold; a flash, and the street. A touch at any moment goes straight to the flash.
- Freedie herself, in blocks: stone shoulders and fists, brick forearms, shins and jaw,
  planks for sinews, rusty rods out of her back, eyes like coals and glowing knees; a black
  dress in tiers with lace, a white apron and bib with buttons, a sash, a black bow.
- Sound, synthesized: wind over the town, the swing, the blow, her pieces knocking down,
  a golden chime for the name; a click for the red button.
- The town behind the street: blocks of houses with red tiled roofs and chimneys.

## 0.2

  bars and a dashed middle line; curbs and pavements; to the left a promenade with two
  rows of palms, a stone parapet and the sea beyond, glittering toward the sun; to the
  right old seaside houses with shutters, iron balconies with flower pots, awnings over
  the shops, cafe parasols.
- Palms of blocks: a trunk ringed with old leaf bases, a crown of fronds whose leaflets

  the house fronts.
- Lamp posts and bollards along the curbs; on one post a call box whose red button glows,
  and lights up when pressed. What it calls comes later.
- Sedans parked along both curbs, cones by the plinth; now and then a car comes down the
  road, stops short of the plinth, turns round and drives off.
- An evening sky warm toward the low sun, with streaks of pink cloud; a warm haze far off.
