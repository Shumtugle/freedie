# Changelog

## 0.21.1
- Things cross the sky again, as seldom as they did in the street game this one grows from:
  now and then a small flock of birds far off over the end of the street, wings beating and
  now and then gliding, and more seldom still an airliner high up, a bright dot drawing a
  white trail that spreads and fades over half a minute. The sky draws them as part of
  itself, so they show over open sky only, behind the palms and the houses.

## 0.21
- The first film between two episodes, after the 39th building: out of the light the street
  is seen from low by the curb, a tower crane on the promenade lowers its hook, the slings
  take the empty plinth, and it is carried three meters down the road, swaying under the
  slewing jib, while the eye goes up and round with it. It is set down with a thud and a
  puff of dust, the slings drop, and the eye comes back down to the player.
- The first time only, the hook comes down once more with a golden cube on a short chain;
  the cube leaves it and flies up into the rack in the crest. After that the film plays
  without it, and a touch cuts it short.
- Two new sounds, both synthesized: the winch's motor, and the hook's chain.

## 0.20
- The fourth episode, whole: the game goes on to the fiftieth building, by the same seeds
  as the street game it grows from, and holds there until the alley is open. Nothing new
  goes into its buildings; the plinth goes farther off, twelve meters at the fortieth and
  twenty-four at the fiftieth, and the close view closes in that much more.
- In the close view the haze thins as if the building had come nearer, so a far one stays
  readable instead of going pale.

## 0.19.3
- Nothing that only goes about the street passes through things any more. A car coming
  down the road stops short of the plinth wherever it stands now, and short of anything of
  the building lying on the road, with room to turn round; the lanes keep clear of the cars
  parked at the curb. People step round palm trunks, lamp posts, bollards, café tables and
  whatever lies about. Weather is the only thing that passes through.

## 0.19.2
- Car alarms are not all the same any more: each parked car has its own, chosen once, the
  two hoarse tones of before, a lock's short double chirp (the commonest), a horn honking
  three times, or a wailing siren.
- The balls are quieter. A rolling ball's rumble no longer starts again every half second
  over the last one (it is one rumble at a time now, and softer); a heavy ball's knocks are
  half as loud; a balloon pops at half the loudness.

## 0.19.1
- In the opening, when the square goes dark and only Freedie is left in her pool of light,
  the people go into the dark with the town: one walking behind her is no longer lit by the
  pool, so no plain figure of a few boxes shows on the black.

## 0.19
- The close view for far plinths. From the 21st building on the plinth stands farther down
  the street; now, while a throw is aimed and while it flies, the view turns straight onto
  the building and closes in on it in a third of a second, the farther the plinth the more
  (by the ratio of the distances, a little less, from one and a quarter to four and a half
  times), and opens out again once the hammer is back in hand.
- The ring stays on the same spot of the building as the view closes in; a finger moved in
  the close view moves the aim finely. The hammer in hand keeps its place and size in the
  frame, and the throw and the catch go from the hand where it is seen.

## 0.18
- The third episode, whole: the game goes on to the thirty-ninth building, as the street
  game it grows from has it, by the same seeds. Its buildings put everything of the second
  episode together and higher: sandbag walls, water bottles under slabs, polished balls on
  pads and in a heap, balloons under slabs, stacks of suitcases and mattresses, drums and
  tyres, and towers with a cone on top. At the thirty-ninth the light rises over the sky,
  as at the end of every episode; after it the last building comes round again until the
  next one is open.

## 0.17
- The fight has its own screen on the bridge, the last on the ring: a black and white
  monitor where Freedie comes up a grey street in leaps, the only colour her golden fire,
  and a car comes tumbling at the glass. Lit from the start; a golden cube in its slot
  opens it for good, and from then on a poke at it starts the fight, as often as the player
  likes. The console has no monitor for the fight any more: the alley and the stones only.
- The brackets round her weak points are gone; her eyes and knees show by their own glow.
- Waking after a fight lost, the street is out of focus at first and comes clear as the
  eyelids part.

## 0.16.1
- The fight reads now. The ring comes up right over the finger and follows it, in the
  diamond's colours with a dark edge, and turns red when it has a car in the air; a car
  within reach of it is taken and led by its flight. No wind-up: the diamond hammer always
  flies hard and flat.
- A finger may aim while the hammer is still on its way back: lifted early, the throw goes
  the moment the hammer is in hand.
- Brackets round her weak points still whole, two knees and the head, and round every car
  in her hand or in the air. A blow that lands bursts where it lands: wide and white-blue
  on a weak point, orange off a car, grey on her bulk; a weak point broken flares white in
  the corner. Her eyes and knees throb and keep their own colours through the red.
- The diamond hammer keeps its pale blue through the red world.

## 0.16
- The fight with Freedie. Once the golden hammer has been given, the next ten buildings lost
  in a row bring her instead: the gods frown and drop a diamond hammer from the thunderer's
  cloud, and the view comes down into the street seen red and black, the plinth gone, and
  Freedie far up the road, half again as tall as in the opening, burning in a golden fire.
  The fight can also be had at once from the bridge: the middle monitor of the console.
- She comes in leaps: crouches, springs, comes down so the street jolts and the dust flies,
  and between leaps tears a parked car from the curb, lifts it over her head and throws it.
  A car knocked out of the air goes up and over to the curb; one that gets through breaks
  a shield, and when the three are gone the heart is heard, and the next one ends it. Time
  runs slow while a car is about to arrive.
- The diamond hammer flies hard and flat and comes straight back. Her weak points glow: a
  knee struck goes dark and her leaps grow short; both, and she drags herself on her knees;
  her eyes go from yellow through orange to red, and the third blow to the head breaks her
  apart. A blow to her bulk knocks a brick out and stops her for a moment.
- Won: the heap trembles a while, the world goes gold and up into the sky, where all the
  gods laugh aloud and give the heavy hammer; then the next building. Lost (a car through
  with no shield left, or she reaches the player): it goes dark, and the player wakes on the
  street before the same building, the eyelids parting.
- Parked cars thrown in the fight lie where they fell as wrecks until the fight is over.

## 0.15
- The polished balls are true spheres now, worked out pixel by pixel on the graphics chip:
  each in one of six deep colours, with a paler marbled swirl and three finger holes that
  turn with the ball as it rolls, and a small hard shine of the low sun with the sky in the
  polish. Before, a ball was five round slices stacked up and read as a striped toy ball.
- Balloons are spheres too: plain rubber, no holes, a broader and softer shine.
- Balls hide and touch each other as round things do, and their shadows are round.

## 0.14
- The second episode, whole: the game goes to the twentieth building. Its things come in
  one after another, each out of boxes: steel drums with their chimes and hoops and a bung
  in the lid; car tyres, tread and sidewalls round a hole; sandbags rounded off and tied at
  one end; old suitcases with straps, brass corners, latches and a handle; spring mattresses
  between concrete slabs; heavy polished balls; balloons that drift off when freed.
- A suitcase that lands hard, or is sent flying, bursts: the lid swings up on its hinge, the
  lining shows, and shirts and postcards fly out. The shirts are as big as real ones, with
  sleeves, a white collar and a big flower print; they swing down slowly, turning over, and
  lie flat where they fall. The postcards side-slip like leaves.
- The mattresses are thick, in broad blue and cream ticking, with dark piping round the
  edges and buttons in rows; under a load the middle sinks. Struck hard, one tears: a dark
  hole with grey wadding round it, springs jumping out of it and flying off.
- Every thing has its voice: drums clang, tyres thud, sandbags thump and puff dust, leather
  slaps, a mattress whumps and creaks, latches snap, cloth tears, balloons pop, a ball
  rumbles as it rolls.
- Nobody walks on the road any more: the people who crossed the street far off are gone,
  and on the square of the opening everybody keeps short of the curbs.

## 0.13
- The parked cars take what falls on them: the hammer, blocks, boards, stones, balls. A
  light knock only clatters; a real blow crumples metal, sprays glass and sets the alarm
  off, the indicators blinking. Each blow is a step worse: crazed glass and a dented boot
  and hood; then the roof pressed lower, a side window gone, the back dented, a headlamp
  out; then the rear window gone and a door stove in. The cars stay as dented as they are.
- A parked car is a thing to aim at: the ring settles on it as on the building.
- On the right, a stretch of the curb is kept clear of parked cars: the way into a yard
  is to be there.

## 0.12
- Episodes: the levels are played as the game's episodes, the first from 1 to 9, the second
  from 10 to 20. Down the last building of an episode and, up among the gods, a light rises
  and whitens the sky, and the next episode opens out of it. A run through an episode from
  its first building on keeps its stars; the bridge knows which episodes are done.
- The bridge takes you into an episode: a screen with its cube, poked, and the game goes
  through the light onto that episode's first building.
- The second episode opens with a road cone on the tenth building: a dark square foot,
  orange and white bands narrowing upward, a plastic knock. The game goes as far as the
  eleventh building for now; the next ones bring tyres, a drum and a mattress, suitcases,
  sandbags and balls, one at a time.

## 0.11
- The top of the frame is a crest. In the middle a medallion with the level's number; its
  colour is the tries at this level, ripening from green through amber to red, and a ring of
  ten segments round it counts them, the newest flashing as it lights; with two left it
  beats. The stars on the left wing, the outermost spent first: each flares white and
  scatters sparks as it burns, the last one left glows. The heavy throws under them. On the
  right wing the way through the level fills toward a flag, a spark at its edge; reached, a
  flash runs along it, it turns green and the flag waves. The golden cubes under it catch a
  glint now and then.
- The golden hammer: lose the same building ten times running and the gods drop it from
  the thunderer's cloud. Back on the street the medallion and its ring turn to gold; one
  golden throw scatters the building whatever it hits, and the count starts again.
- A new building starts the count of tries afresh; the game keeps it between games.

## 0.10
- The corner, as in the game this one grows from: five stars along the top left, one burnt
  a throw, flashing when a building comes down; the golden cubes in the rack beside them;
  on the right the weight still on the plinth with the line it must fall below, and the
  level's number on its plate.
- Run out of stars and the gods frown, anger, sleep and doubt, and the same building comes
  down out of the sky again for another try.
- The first building is the gods' welcome: it stands close, flies to pieces at the first
  touch, they all laugh and give the heavy hammer. From the second on the plinth stands a
  little further off and the game is played fair.
- The heavy hammer: a building felled at one throw brings three heavy throws, nine at most.
  The gift drops from the thunderer's cloud past the gods; back on the street its marks drop
  in under the stars one by one. A dark wrought head bound in bronze, slower off the hand,
  far heavier on the building. A tap on the hammer in hand or on the one waiting low on the
  left swaps them. The game keeps the heavy throws left.
- The cost of a frame moved to the bottom left, out of the stars' way.

## 0.9
- The sky with the gods: a building down, and once all is still the eye tips up into the
  evening sky. Far clouds hang there and the old gods of the place sail in, each in a cloud
  of his own; their faces come out of the vapour, a wisp of haze passes, and they judge:
  laughter and the thunderer's lightning, with thunder rolling in after, for a building felled
  at one throw; smiles for a few throws; a sceptic look for many. The first building is their
  welcome: they all laugh aloud.
- Then the next building falls out of the sky onto the plinth, block by block from high up,
  as the eye comes back down. The levels go on to the ninth, the end of the first run; the
  ninth comes round again until the stars come. The game keeps the level it is on.
- People: up and down the pavement under the houses, along the promenade, across the road
  far off; and in the opening, crossing the square behind Freedie and along its sides. Close
  up a person of blocks reads as a toy, so nobody comes nearer to the eye than a few meters.

## 0.8
- The first level: the building comes down onto the plinth block by block, the same first
  building as in the game this one grows from, wooden posts and boards and two bottles of
  water. The wooden hammer is in hand, low on the right.
- Put a finger down anywhere and a ring comes up on the building; move it and the ring
  moves along, never under the finger; hold still and the hammer draws back, a band fills
  round the ring and the throw gathers strength; hold too long and the ring shakes. The
  dotted arc shows the flight. Let go and it flies: a quick release lobs, a full one flies
  flat, spinning about the upright, and comes back to the hand.
- The first building flies to pieces at the first touch of the hammer, with thunder.
  Pieces knock with the voices of wood and stone, chips and dust fly, bottles burst into
  spray and leave puddles that dry up. Parked cars stand in the way.
- Three quarters of the building off the plinth, and once all is still what is left goes
  in a puff and the building comes down again. Stars and further levels are next.
- Freedie opens every game again; her heart, the golden cube, rises only at the first
  meeting. The name on the square is gone.
- The hammer thrown at the call box in the first game of the bridge flies the same way as
  on the street, flat and spinning, in an arc under the ground's pull.
- Round things in the engine: bottles and puddles are twelve-sided, out of plain boxes.

## 0.7.1
- In the first game of the bridge the hammer is in hand: the wooden one from the street,
  low on the right, its head across the view, swaying with every hop.
- The call box is answered with it: a tap on the red button throws the hammer, end over
  end, the button goes in, the box rings and shakes, the hammer drops onto the stone, and
  the dispatcher takes you back to the bridge. Bouncing on the box no longer calls.
- The game there goes on without end, so its golden cubes are rare now: the second level
  still has one, and after it one level in every twenty-five, never two close together.

## 0.7
- The first game of the bridge is played: its screen is lit from the start, showing islands
  going away over the dark and a cube turning over the far one, and a poke at it goes into
  the hopscotch over the quicksilver, whole, with its own levels, sound and settings.
- There the player is a diamond hammer, not a drop: clear and cold, with darker bands where
  the head is set; the mirages show the hammer too.
- In some levels one of the shards is a golden cube, turning and glowing. Taken, it goes
  home to the rack on the bridge; each such level gives its cube once.
- Every level has an island with a call box on it, a red button on a post. Bounce on it
  three times, a ring each time, and the dispatcher takes you back to the bridge, with the
  cubes found there.
- The rivals and the black drop are still drops.

## 0.6
- The first game opens with Freedie, once: out of what is left of her after the blow rises
  her heart, a golden cube, turning and glowing in the dark, and comes to rest under her
  name. It is her gift, the first cube, and it is in the first slot of the bridge already.
- Every game after opens with the name on the square: the eye comes down over the town,
  the blocks of FREEDIE come down out of the sky onto a long pedestal letter by letter, the
  hammer comes up in the hand and flies, the name flies to pieces, the eye glides down onto
  them, and a burst of light carries it into the game. A touch goes straight on.
- On the bridge the hammer is held like a hand: a tap pokes what is under the finger, out
  from the lower right, a knock, and back.
- The desktop stand can start the game again with what it kept (`restart`).

## 0.5
- The red button calls the dispatcher: each press rings, and three in a row open the
  bridge, the same room as in the game this one grows from: a ring of old sets, one for
  each episode, turned by a swipe and settling with a click; a slot under each, where a
  golden cube goes in for good; the console with its monitors, the dial and the HOME
  lever. A throw at an open screen goes through the light back to the street, as there are
  no levels yet; the monitors of the alley, the golem and the stones stay dark for now.
  The lever, or the back key, switches the room off like a set, and the street comes back
  on from a bright line.
- The sets show stills from the game this one grows from, until this one has its own.
- Golden cubes and the farthest level are kept between games.

## 0.3
- The opening: the eye flies in an arc high over the seaside town and comes down onto the
  street before Freedie, who raises her eyes; the town goes dark, light left on her alone;
  she swings her golden hammer straight at the eye, the glass cracks, and she breaks apart
  and falls, every piece of her a body of the game's own physics; on the black the name
  burns in gold; a flash, and the street. A touch at any moment goes straight to the flash.
- Freedie herself, in blocks: stone shoulders and fists, brick forearms, shins and jaw,
  planks for sinews, rusty rods out of her back, eyes like coals and glowing knees; a black
  dress in tiers with lace, a white apron and bib with buttons, a sash, a black bow.
- Sound, synthesized: wind over the town, the swing, the blow, her pieces knocking down,
  a golden chime for the name; a click for the red button.
- The town behind the street: blocks of houses with red tiled roofs and chimneys.

## 0.2

  bars and a dashed middle line; curbs and pavements; to the left a promenade with two
  rows of palms, a stone parapet and the sea beyond, glittering toward the sun; to the

- Palms of blocks: a trunk ringed with old leaf bases, a crown of fronds whose leaflets

- Lamp posts and bollards along the curbs; on one post a call box whose red button glows,

- Sedans parked along both curbs, cones by the plinth; now and then a car comes down the
  road, stops short of the plinth, turns round and drives off.
- An evening sky warm toward the low sun, with streaks of pink cloud; a warm haze far off.
